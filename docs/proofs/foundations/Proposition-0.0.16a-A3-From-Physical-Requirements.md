**Corollary:** The B₃ and C₃ root lattices are eliminated by these physical requirements.

## 2. Background: Root Lattice Classification

### 2.1 Rank-3 Root Lattices

By the classification of root systems (Dynkin, 1947, Uspekhi Mat. Nauk 2(4):59-127; see Humphreys, 1972, Chapter III), the irreducible rank-3 root lattices are:

| Lattice | Associated Algebra | Root Lattice Q | Coord. of Q | Simply-Laced? |
|---------|-------------------|----------------|-------------|---------------|
| **A₃** | $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ | FCC (D₃) | 12 | Yes |
| **B₃** | $\mathfrak{so}(7)$ | $\mathbb{Z}^3$ (simple cubic) | 6 | No |
| **C₃** | $\mathfrak{sp}(6)$ | FCC (D₃) = Q(A₃) | 12 | No |

> **Note on D₃:** The D₃ root system is isomorphic to A₃ (since $\mathfrak{so}(6) \cong \mathfrak{su}(4)$), so it need not be considered separately.

> **Root lattice vs weight lattice (CRITICAL DISTINCTION):** The coordination numbers above are for the **root lattice** Q, which is the lattice generated by root vectors and governs gauge interactions. These differ from the **weight lattice** P:
> - B₃: Q(B₃) = $\mathbb{Z}^3$ (coord. 6), but P(B₃) = BCC (coord. 8)
> - C₃: Q(C₃) = FCC (coord. 12), but P(C₃) = $\mathbb{Z}^3$ (coord. 6)
>
> The physical lattice in the framework corresponds to the root lattice Q (generated by gauge interaction vectors), not the weight lattice P.

### 2.2 Scope: Irreducible Root Lattices Only

> **Note on Reducible Extensions:** We consider only **irreducible** rank-3 root lattices. Reducible extensions like A₂ ⊕ A₁ (rank-3 but decomposable) are excluded by the physical requirements:
> - **Vertex-transitivity** (Theorem 0.0.6): Every vertex must have the same local structure
> - **Space-filling** (Theorem 0.0.6): The tiling must fill all of 3D without gaps
> - A reducible lattice A₂ ⊕ A₁ would have inequivalent directions, violating vertex-transitivity
>
> Non-crystallographic extensions are similarly excluded by the requirement of exact phase coherence across the lattice.

### 2.3 The A₂ Sublattice Question

For A₂ (SU(3) weight lattice) to embed as a **root sublattice** of a rank-3 lattice L:
- L must contain a 2D hyperplane with the hexagonal A₂ structure
- The stacking direction perpendicular to this plane must be compatible with L's structure
- **Root length ratios must be preserved** (critical for Lie-algebraic structure)

## 3. Proof

### 3.1 Part (a): The Third Dimension is Required

**Claim:** Physical space must have dimension 3, not 2.

**Proof:**

From Physical Hypothesis 0.0.0f (Definition 0.0.0 §4.6):
> For field dynamics to support a radial confinement direction, the embedding dimension satisfies:
> $$d_{\text{embed}} = d_{\text{weight}} + 1 = \text{rank}(G) + 1$$

For SU(3): rank(SU(3)) = 2, therefore:
$$d_{\text{embed}} = 2 + 1 = 3$$

The A₂ weight space is 2-dimensional. The physical requirement of confinement (flux tube formation, radial pressure) demands a third direction perpendicular to the weight plane.

$\blacksquare$ (Part a)

---

### 3.2 Part (b): The Perpendicular Direction is Fixed

**Claim:** The third direction must be along the [111] axis (perpendicular to the A₂ plane).

**Proof:**

From Theorem 0.0.3 (Stella Uniqueness):
- The stella octangula is the unique minimal 3D geometric realization of SU(3)
- It has exactly 2 apex vertices (§2.2, "Claim: Exactly 2 apex vertices are required")
- These apexes lie at positions $\pm\vec{a}$ where $\vec{a}$ is perpendicular to the weight plane

The apex-to-apex axis defines the radial/confinement direction. In standard FCC coordinates, this is the [111] direction:
$$\hat{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1, 1, 1)$$

The A₂ weight plane is the hyperplane $x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 0$, which is perpendicular to [111].

$\blacksquare$ (Part b)

---

### 3.3 Part (c): The Stacking Pattern is Determined

**Claim:** The A₂ layers must stack to form the FCC (A₃) lattice, not any other arrangement.

**Proof:**

From Theorem 0.0.6 (Spatial Extension from Octet Truss):

**Lemma 0.0.6a** establishes that the tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb is the **unique vertex-transitive** edge-to-edge tiling by regular tetrahedra and octahedra.

**Lemma 0.0.6b** establishes that each vertex of this honeycomb has exactly 8 tetrahedra meeting, forming a stella octangula.

**Lemma 0.0.6c** establishes that the vertex set of this honeycomb is precisely the FCC lattice:
$$\Lambda_{\text{FCC}} = \{(n_1, n_2, n_3) \in \mathbb{Z}^3 : n_1 + n_2 + n_3 \equiv 0 \pmod{2}\}$$

The FCC lattice is isomorphic to the A₃ root lattice. The A₂ planes appear as cross-sections at fixed values of $n_1 + n_2 + n_3$.

**Key observation:** The stacking is not arbitrary. For the tetrahedra to share faces coherently (phase coherence condition, Theorem 0.0.6 part c), the layers must be offset by specific vectors. These offsets are precisely those that generate the FCC structure from stacked hexagonal layers.

> **Why FCC (ABCABC) and not HCP (ABAB)?**
>
> Both FCC and HCP are close-packings of spheres with the same coordination number 12. However:
> - **HCP** (hexagonal close-packing) has **ABAB** stacking, where every other layer is directly above the first
> - **FCC** (face-centered cubic) has **ABCABC** stacking, where layers cycle through three positions
>
> The distinction is forced by **edge-to-edge tiling** (Theorem 0.0.6 Lemma 0.0.6a):
> - The tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb requires tetrahedra to share complete faces
> - HCP stacking produces a honeycomb with different local structure at alternating layers
> - Only FCC stacking produces the **vertex-transitive** honeycomb where every vertex has identical local structure
>
> Since vertex-transitivity is required for universal SU(3) phase coherence (every lattice site must be equivalent), FCC is uniquely selected.

$\blacksquare$ (Part c)

### 3.4 Part (d): Elimination of B₃ and C₃

**Claim:** The B₃ and C₃ root systems fail to satisfy the physical requirements.

**Proof:**

We show that B₃ is eliminated at the lattice level (wrong coordination), while C₃ produces the same lattice as A₃ but is eliminated at the Lie-algebraic level (not simply-laced, non-uniform gauge coupling).

#### B₃ Elimination

The B₃ root system has 18 roots: $\pm e_i$ (6 short roots, length 1) and $\pm e_i \pm e_j$ for $i \neq j$ (12 long roots, length $\sqrt{2}$).

The root lattice $Q(B_3)$ is the integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^3$ (simple cubic), with:
- **Coordination number:** 6 (nearest neighbors are $\pm e_i$ at distance 1)
- **Vertex figure:** The 6 neighbors form an **octahedron**
- **Root structure:** Not simply-laced (root lengths 1 and $\sqrt{2}$)

**Failure mode 1 (Coordination):** Theorem 0.0.16 Part (a) derives that the lattice must have coordination 12 (6 intra-representation + 6 inter-representation edges). $Q(B_3)$ has coordination 6. Eliminated.

**Failure mode 2 (Stella structure):** An octahedral vertex figure (6 vertices) cannot support a stella octangula (which requires 8 vertices partitioned into two interpenetrating tetrahedra). Eliminated.

**Failure mode 3 (Tiling):** The simple cubic lattice $\mathbb{Z}^3$ does not support a tetrahedral-octahedral honeycomb. Its natural tiling is by cubes, not tetrahedra and octahedra. Eliminated.

> **Note:** A₂ does embed among the **long** roots of B₃ (the 12 roots $\pm e_i \pm e_j$ include the A₂ root vectors $\pm(e_i - e_j)$). However, this embedding is irrelevant because B₃ is already eliminated by lattice coordination alone.

#### C₃ Resolution

The C₃ root system has 18 roots: $\pm(e_i - e_j)$ for $i \neq j$ (12 short roots, length $\sqrt{2}$) and $\pm 2e_i$ (6 long roots, length 2).

The root lattice $Q(C_3) = \{(n_1, n_2, n_3) \in \mathbb{Z}^3 : n_1 + n_2 + n_3 \equiv 0 \pmod{2}\} = D_3 \cong A_3$ (FCC lattice), with:
- **Coordination number:** 12 (same as A₃)
- **Vertex figure:** cuboctahedron (same as A₃)

**Key observation:** $Q(C_3) = Q(A_3)$ as lattices. Just as $D_3 \cong A_3$ is handled by lattice isomorphism (§2.1), C₃ produces the **same physical lattice** as A₃. At the lattice level, C₃ is not "eliminated" — it is **redundant**.

**However, the Lie-algebraic structure differs critically:**

| Property | A₃ ($\mathfrak{su}(4)$) | C₃ ($\mathfrak{sp}(6)$) |
|----------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Root lengths | All $\sqrt{2}$ (uniform) | $\sqrt{2}$ and $2$ (two scales) |
| Simply-laced | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| Nearest-neighbor roots | 12 (all equivalent) | 12 short roots only |
| Additional roots | None | 6 long roots ($\pm 2e_i$) at distance 2 |

**Why C₃ is eliminated (Lie-algebraic argument):**

1. **Non-uniform gauge coupling:** The 6 long roots $\pm 2e_i$ of C₃ correspond to second-nearest-neighbor vectors in the FCC lattice (at distance 2, not $\sqrt{2}$). A C₃-based gauge theory would introduce gauge interactions at two distinct distance scales, creating anisotropic coupling incompatible with the uniform SU(3) phase coherence required by Theorem 0.0.6.

2. **Simply-laced preservation:** A₂ is simply-laced (all roots length $\sqrt{2}$). While A₂ embeds among the **short** roots of C₃ (all 6 A₂ root vectors $\pm(e_i - e_j)$ have length $\sqrt{2}$ and lie among the 12 short C₃ roots), the additional 6 long roots of C₃ have no counterpart in the A₂ structure. Extending A₂ to A₃ preserves the simply-laced property; extending to C₃ does not.

3. **Gauge group determination:** The framework derives SU(3) from the stella octangula (Theorem 0.0.3). The rank-3 extension must be compatible with the gauge group structure. A₃ = $\mathfrak{su}(4)$ is the natural extension (SU(3) ⊂ SU(4) via fundamental embedding). C₃ = $\mathfrak{sp}(6)$ would require a fundamentally different gauge group (Sp(6)) with no natural connection to the SU(3) structure already established.

**Conclusion:** The lattice $Q(C_3) = Q(A_3)$ = FCC is the correct physical lattice, but the associated Lie algebra must be A₃ ($\mathfrak{su}(4)$, simply-laced) rather than C₃ ($\mathfrak{sp}(6)$, not simply-laced), to preserve uniform gauge coupling and compatibility with SU(3).

$\blacksquare$ (Part d)
